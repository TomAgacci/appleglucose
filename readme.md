Here's some instructions

APPLE PULP → GLUCOSE-RICH SYRUP → CIDER → FRUCTOSE-ENRICHED EXTRACT

PART 1 — GLUCOSE-RICH SYRUP FROM APPLE PULP

INGREDIENTS:
- Apple pulp
- Water
- Lemon juice (citric acid source)

EQUIPMENT:
- Pot
- Thermometer (optional but ideal)
- Stirring utensil
- Fine mesh / cloth / nut milk bag
- Stove or heat source
- Glass jar(s)

STEP 1 — MAKE SLURRY
- Add apple pulp to a pot.
- Add 1–1.5 parts water by volume relative to pulp.
- Stir until you have a thick, uniform slurry.

STEP 2 — ACIDIFY
- Add 1–2 tablespoons lemon juice per liter of slurry.
- This lowers pH (approx. 3.0–3.5) and helps mild hydrolysis of pectin/starch.

STEP 3 — HEAT FOR HYDROLYSIS
- Heat slurry to 80–90 °C.
- Hold at this temperature for 45–60 minutes.
- Stir every 5–10 minutes.
- Goal: extract native sugars and partially break down complex carbs.

STEP 4 — STRAIN
- Pour hot slurry through fine mesh or cloth.
- Squeeze/press to extract as much liquid as possible.
- Collect the strained liquid in a clean pot.

STEP 5 — CONCENTRATE TO SYRUP
- Heat strained liquid on LOW.
- Simmer gently, avoiding hard boiling.
- Reduce volume by ~30–60% until syrupy.
- Cool and transfer to a clean glass jar.
- This is your GLUCOSE-RICH SYRUP.

PART 2 — FERMENT GLUCOSE-RICH SYRUP INTO CIDER

INGREDIENTS:
- Glucose-rich syrup
- Water (to dilute to cider-like sugar level)
- Wine/cider yeast (or baker’s yeast for simple tests)

EQUIPMENT:
- Fermentation vessel (bottle/jug with airlock or loose lid)
- Airlock or loosely fitted lid
- Sanitized spoon

STEP 1 — DILUTE SYRUP
- Mix syrup with water to reach ~10–15% sugar by weight (roughly 1 part syrup to 2–3 parts water, depending on thickness).
- Stir until fully dissolved.

STEP 2 — COOL & PITCH YEAST
- Ensure liquid is at room temperature (~20–25 °C).
- Add yeast according to package instructions (typically 1–5 g per 1–5 L).
- Stir gently to distribute.

STEP 3 — FERMENT
- Fit airlock or loosely cover the vessel.
- Ferment at 18–24 °C.
- Active fermentation: 3–7 days (bubbling).
- Secondary clearing: 7–14 days (slower activity, sediment forming).
- When bubbling stops and liquid clears, you have CIDER.

STEP 4 — RACK (OPTIONAL)
- Carefully pour cider off sediment into a clean container.
- This reduces solids and improves clarity.

PART 3 — FRUCTOSE-ENRICHED SUGAR EXTRACT (FREEZE CONCENTRATION)

METHOD A — FREEZE CONCENTRATION (ICE-CIDER STYLE)

STEP 1 — FREEZE
- Pour finished cider into a freezer-safe container.
- Freeze solid.

STEP 2 — SLOW THAW
- Move frozen cider to the refrigerator.
- Let it thaw slowly.
- As it thaws, liquid will drip out first.

STEP 3 — COLLECT FIRST MELT
- Collect the first 30–50% of melted liquid.
- This fraction is higher in sugars and flavor (fructose-enriched relative to the original cider).
- Remaining ice is mostly water.

METHOD B — OPTIONAL HEAT REDUCTION

STEP 4 — REDUCE (OPTIONAL)
- Take the enriched fraction.
- Heat gently at 60–70 °C.
- Evaporate water slowly until syrupy.
- Avoid boiling hard to prevent caramelization.

OUTPUTS:
- GLUCOSE-RICH SYRUP (from pulp).
- CIDER (from fermenting syrup).
- FRUCTOSE-ENRICHED SUGAR EXTRACT (from freeze concentration and optional reduction).

NOTE:
- All products are mixtures of sugars (glucose, fructose, sucrose).
- None of these steps produce isolated pure fructose or HFCS.
